A beginner-friendly, step-by-step walkthrough to configure Direct Upload to Google Play Console and Apple App Store Connect directly from your ieWebNative Builder.

How Direct Store Publishing Works

Normally, after building your app, you would have to download large binary files (.aab or .ipa) and manually upload them into the developer consoles.

With Direct Upload, the ieWebNative Cloud Builder compiles, signs, packages, and securely communicates with Google's and Apple's developer APIs to deliver your release straight to your store account in one single build step.

Security Guarantee: Your API keys (JSON / .p8) are used strictly in-memory during build execution for authenticating with Google / Apple official APIs and are protected with end-to-end encryption.

Google Play Console Setup (Android)

Requires Service Account JSON Key
1

Register & Create an App in Google Play Console

  1. Visit the Google Play Console and sign in with your Google Account.
  2. Complete the developer account registration ($25 USD one-time fee).
  3. In your dashboard, click Create app.
  4. Provide your App name, select App, and choose Free or Paid.
  5. Important: Note down your Package Name (e.g. com.company.myapp). The package name in the ieWebNative Builder under App Details must match this exactly.
2

Enable Google Play Android Developer API

  1. Open the Google Cloud Console .
  2. Select your existing project or create a new project named Play Console Publisher.
  3. In the top search bar, type Google Play Android Developer API.
  4. Click the API from the search results and click the blue Enable button.
3

Create Service Account & Generate JSON Key

  1. In Google Cloud Console, open the left navigation menu and navigate to IAM & Admin → Service Accounts.
  2. Click + CREATE SERVICE ACCOUNT at the top.
  3. Enter a Service account name (e.g. play-publisher) and click Create and Continue.
  4. In the Role dropdown, you can select Service Account User (or skip role assignment as permissions will be granted directly inside Play Console). Click Done.
  5. In the Service Accounts list, click on the email of the service account you just created.
  6. Navigate to the Keys tab at the top.
  7. Click ADD KEY → Create new key.
  8. Ensure JSON is selected, then click Create.
  9. Your browser will immediately download a file named like project-name-12345.json.
This is your Google Service Account JSON Key! Save this file on your computer. You will upload this exact file in the ieWebNative Builder under Store Publishing → Google Service Account JSON Key.
4

Grant Service Account Permissions in Play Console

  1. Return to your Google Play Console .
  2. In the left sidebar, click Users and permissions.
  3. Click Invite new users.
  4. Paste the email address of your Service Account (e.g. play-publisher@your-project.iam.gserviceaccount.com).
  5. Under App permissions, click Add app and select your app.
  6. Check the following permissions:
    • Manage testing tracks (Internal, Closed, Open)
    • Release apps to testing tracks
    • Manage production releases (if deploying directly to production)
  7. Click Apply, then click Invite user at the bottom.
5

Upload & Publish in ieWebNative Builder

  1. Open the ieWebNative Builder .
  2. Set your target platform to Android or Android (AAB).
  3. Under Store Publishing:
    • Check Direct Upload to Google Play Console
    • Click to upload your downloaded .json Service Account key.
    • Select your Release Track (We recommend Internal Testing for quick testing without review delays).
    • Select your Release Status (We recommend Draft so you can review rollout in Play Console).
  4. Click Build App in the top toolbar. Your release bundle will compile and upload automatically!

Apple App Store Connect Setup (iOS)

Requires .p8 API Key, Key ID & Issuer ID
1

Enroll in the Apple Developer Program

  1. Visit Apple Developer Program .
  2. Enroll as an Individual or Organization ($99 USD / year).
2

Create App Record in App Store Connect

  1. Go to App Store Connect → Apps .
  2. Click the + button and choose New App.
  3. Select iOS, enter your App Name, primary language, and choose your Bundle ID.
  4. Enter an SKU (internal tracking ID, e.g. app-001), and click Create.
3

Generate App Store Connect API Key (.p8)

  1. In App Store Connect, click Users and Access in the top menu.
  2. Click the Integrations tab (or Keys).
  3. Under App Store Connect API, click Generate API Key (or the + icon).
  4. Enter a Key Name (e.g. ieWebNative Publisher).
  5. Set the Access Role: Choose App Manager or Developer.
  6. Click Generate.
  7. Your new key will appear in the table. Click Download API Key.
Download Immediately: Apple only lets you download this .p8 file once! Store it securely in a safe location.
4

Locate Key ID and Issuer ID

You need two additional identifiers displayed on that same page:

  • Key ID: A 10-character alphanumeric string (e.g. 2X9R427AD2) shown right next to your key in the list.
  • Issuer ID: A UUID (e.g. 57246542-96fe-1a63-e053-0824d011072a) displayed at the very top of the Keys page.
5

Upload & Publish in ieWebNative Builder

  1. Select iOS as target platform in ieWebNative Builder.
  2. In the Apple Signing section, upload your:
    • Distribution Certificate (.p12) and password
    • App Store Provisioning Profile (.mobileprovision)
  3. In the Store Publishing section:
    • Check Direct Upload to App Store Connect / TestFlight
    • Upload your downloaded AuthKey_XXXXXX.p8 file.
    • Paste your 10-character Key ID.
    • Paste your Issuer ID UUID.
  4. Click Build App. The Cloud Builder signs your .ipa and delivers it directly to App Store Connect / TestFlight!

Troubleshooting & FAQs

Error 403: The caller does not have permission (Google Play)
This occurs when the Service Account was invited to Play Console, but the invite has not been accepted or permissions were not applied. Make sure you went to Google Play Console → Users and permissions, added your service account email, and granted "Release apps to testing tracks" for your specific app. Also allow up to 15 minutes for newly created Google Cloud permissions to propagate.
Apple 401 Unauthorized or Invalid Key ID / Issuer ID
Double-check that the 10-character Key ID and Issuer ID UUID match the exact key downloaded. Also make sure the key role in App Store Connect is set to at least App Manager or Developer so it has permissions to create builds.
Package Name / Bundle ID mismatch
Both Google Play and Apple App Store require the Package Name (Bundle ID) configured in ieWebNative under App Details to match the exact Bundle ID created in the store console (e.g. com.mycompany.app).
How soon do builds appear in TestFlight / Google Play?
On Google Play Internal Testing, the build appears instantly (typically 1-3 minutes). On Apple TestFlight, Apple runs automated processing (symbol extraction and virus scan), which takes approximately 5 to 15 minutes before the build becomes ready to install for testers.
